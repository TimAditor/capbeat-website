The best caption plugins for Premiere Pro — an honest comparison

8 min read · by the CapBeat team

Full disclosure: we make CapBeat, one of the tools below. We'd rather you pick the right tool than pick ours for the wrong job — so this comparison names the cases where the others win. All prices checked August 2026.

Word-by-word captions are the norm on Shorts, Reels and TikTok — and Premiere Pro editors have half a dozen ways to get them. They differ in four things that actually matter: how good the timing is, where your footage gets processed, what it costs over time, and whether they survive contact with music.

Premiere's built-in Speech-to-Text — the baseline

Free, already installed, and genuinely fine for one job: classic sentence-level subtitles on clean speech. Adobe's transcription is solid and runs locally.

  • ✔ Free, integrated, no extra install
  • ✔ Good accuracy on clear spoken language
  • ✘ The animated word-by-word look means manual keyframing or MOGRT workarounds
  • ✘ No answer for music underneath the voice

Pick it when: you need plain subtitles for an interview or documentary and the social-media look doesn't matter.

Captioneer — the established plugin

A well-known extension with template-based caption styling and a lifetime option, which is rare and commendable in this market.

  • ✔ Mature template library
  • ✔ Lifetime license available ($150)
  • ✘ Standard recognition only — struggles when music sits under the voice

Pick it when: you live in template workflows and caption mostly spoken content.

Submagic — the web service

Popular with social teams: upload a clip, get a styled captioned video back. Strong trendy templates, fast results — but it's a web service, with everything that implies.

  • ✔ Very fast to a finished look
  • ✔ Trend templates maintained for you
  • ✘ Your footage leaves your machine — a real problem for NDA and client work
  • ✘ Subscription adds up: three years cost more than any one-time tool
  • ✘ Not inside Premiere — export, upload, re-import

Pick it when: you don't edit in Premiere anyway and the footage isn't sensitive.

CapBeat — ours, and here's the honest pitch

CapBeat exists because of one gap no other Premiere plugin filled: captions for content with music. Speech recognition is trained on spoken language — put a beat under the voice and it hears drums instead of consonants. CapBeat adds a separation step (Demucs, the open-source model by Meta) that splits vocals from instrumental before transcribing, locally on your CPU. On our reference clip that's the difference between 14 recognized words and the complete 47-word lyric.

  • ✔ The only Premiere plugin with built-in vocal separation for lyrics
  • ✔ 100% local — nothing is ever uploaded
  • ✔ Free tier without watermark (up to 10-minute videos, full recognition quality)
  • ✔ €79 one-time for Pro, no subscription; plus 19 drop-in object animations
  • ✘ Windows only for now — the Mac build is in progress
  • ✘ Young product: smaller template library than tools that are years old
  • ✘ Vocal separation takes roughly the video's length to compute

FAQ

Can Premiere Pro caption music videos automatically?

Not reliably with standard tools — recognition guesses once a beat sits under the voice. Separating the vocals first (what CapBeat's music mode does) is currently the only approach that works inside Premiere.

Do caption plugins upload my footage?

Web services do; that's their architecture. Premiere's built-in captions and CapBeat process everything on your machine.
